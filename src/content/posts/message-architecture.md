Most campaigns don't fail because the execution was bad. They fail because there was
never a single idea to execute. Five teams produce five brilliant things that don't
belong to each other, and the audience walks away remembering nothing.

Message architecture is how you avoid that. It's the spine that every channel, asset, and
spokesperson hangs off — decided *before* anyone opens a design file.

## Start with one big idea

Not a tagline. A claim you can defend, and that your audience can repeat in a sentence.
I write it as a full statement, not a slogan, because a statement forces me to be honest:

> *Access to basic financial services shouldn't depend on where you happen to be born.*

Now every asset has a job: prove that, or lose its place.

## Three proof points, maximum

An idea with no evidence is an opinion. So the next layer is the smallest set of facts
that make the big idea true. Three is usually the ceiling — human memory doesn't hold
more, and a fourth point dilutes the first three.

For each proof point, I want:

- **The evidence** — a number, a case, a document.
- **The emotional hook** — why a real person should care.
- **The proof owner** — who is allowed to say it, and where they say it.

## Then, translate for each audience

The big idea stays fixed. The language doesn't. A regulator, a customer, and the person
on your frontline need the same truth in three different vocabularies:

| Audience | What they ask | What you lead with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulator | "Is this compliant and credible?" | Governance and standards |
| Customer | "What's in it for me?" | A concrete, immediate benefit |
| Employee | "Do I believe this?" | Behind-the-scenes honesty |

Same spine, different clothes. That's the whole system.

## Test it before you scale it

Before any spend, I run a comprehension check: show the core message to a handful of
people who aren't in the room, and ask them to repeat it back. If they can't, the message
isn't finished — no matter how good it looks in the deck. Moving comprehension from
"rough idea" to "I could explain this to my neighbour" is worth more than any attention
metric.

## Earned, owned, paid — one story

The reason architecture matters more than ever is that earned, owned, and paid media now
reach the same person in the same hour. If the press release, the LinkedIn post, and the
ad each tell a slightly different story, you're not amplifying a message — you're
cancelling one out. The spine is what makes three channels feel like one voice.

Decide the one idea. Prove it three ways. Translate it without changing it. Everything
else is craft.
