The most common measurement failure I see isn't a lack of data. It's a lack of a *baseline*. Teams measure intensely after a launch and then compare the result to nothing, or to a number someone remembers from last year.

This is an open, ongoing effort to fix that: a simple, comparable baseline for launch campaigns.

## What it records

For every campaign, before the first pitch goes out, I capture four things and freeze them:

- **Coverage volume** — total items, by tier of outlet.
- **Share of voice** — mentions against the closest three competitors.
- **Sentiment mix** — positive / neutral / negative, by the same coding rules every time.
- **Message pull-through** — using the framework from my related research.

## Why a frozen baseline matters

A baseline you can move after the fact isn't a baseline. It's a story. Freezing the numbers *before* the launch removes the temptation to grade on a curve — and it makes the comparison defensible in a boardroom where someone is sceptical of communications spend.

## The measurement frame

| Metric | Captured | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage volume | Pre-launch | Comms lead |
| Share of voice | Pre-launch | Comms lead |
| Sentiment mix | Pre-launch | Analyst |
| Pull-through | Post-launch | Comms lead |

Small enough to run without a dedicated analyst, structured enough to compare across launches.

## Status

This is a working effort, refined across campaigns as I go. The coding rules for sentiment are the part most likely to change — they're the source of most disagreement — and I'm documenting them as edge cases appear so the method stays repeatable for whoever inherits it.
