## The brief

A major palm oil operation needed to connect more than 2,000 plantation workers to the
company's business goals — sustainability commitments, safety standards, corporate
culture — across sites where a glossy intranet meant nothing and a legal misstep in a
safety notice meant real exposure.

The hard part wasn't producing content. It was producing content that survived the trip
from a head office slide deck to a worker standing in a field.

## What I did

- Designed a **monthly internal campaign** built around one human idea per month, not one
  policy memo per month.
- Reworked corporate updates into **simple, human-centred stories** — what's changing,
  why it matters, and what it means for the person reading it.
- Partnered with safety teams to write **field safety and environmental communication**
  that was easy to understand *and* legally sound — approved language, plain delivery.
- Kept every notice **compliant first**: no claim or instruction that could create risk,
  while still sounding like it came from people, not a legal department.
- Built feedback loops so messages that weren't landing could be fixed the next month.

## The outcome

- 2,000+ workers connected to the company's main business goals through stories they
  could actually repeat.
- Safety and sustainability messages that frontline teams trusted — because they were
  written *for* them, not *at* them.
- A repeatable monthly rhythm that internal teams could keep running themselves.

### The principle

Internal communication fails quietly. Nobody complains about it — they just stop reading.
The fix is rarely a better channel. It's a better story for the person on the other end.
